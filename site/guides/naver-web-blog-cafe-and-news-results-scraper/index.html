    Naver Web, Blog, Cafe & News Results Scraper: export to CSV, Excel or JSON

    Search Naver's web, blog, cafe, or news results for a Korean or any-language query, and get back title, URL, domain, snippet, and source name for each organic result, filterable by keyword or domain.

    What people export it for

    • Korean SEO and rank tracking — record which pages and blogs rank for a query, in relevance or newest-first order, with stable ranks and exact URLs
    • Brand and news monitoring — batch a company or product name, restrict to a date window, and keep publisher, section, headline, snippet and publish time
    • Content and RAG datasets — collect full blog and news bodies with authors, images and normalized timestamps
    • Local and place research — pull the place pack from integrated results with ratings, review counts, categories and map links
    • Visual and video research — collect images with original dimensions and videos with duration, views, channel and platform
    • Keyword discovery — expand seed terms with Naver autocomplete suggestions

    Fix common problems

    Do I need a Naver account, cookies or a proxy?

    No. The actor collects public Naver result pages without login, cookies or any proxy configuration.

    Why does webSearch return only a handful of rows?

    Naver's web-document section on the integrated page currently shows roughly 2–17 documents per query and does not paginate. Use integratedSearch to also capture places, ads and AI briefings, or the dedicated blog, cafe, news, image and video modes for deeper verticals.

    Why are some publish dates relative?

    Naver often prints labels such as 3주 전 or 11시간 전. Those rows keep the raw label in date / publishedAtRaw, get a computed publishedAt, and are marked publishedAtPrecision: "relative" so you can treat them differently from exact timestamps.
