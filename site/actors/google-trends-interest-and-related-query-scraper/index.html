    Google Trends Interest & Related Query Scraper is an Apify Actor published by Vexaloom Labs. It accepts 14 input fields, none of them required at the top level. Each result can carry up to 36 declared fields, including recordType, searchTerms, title, geoName, timeRangeLabel and hoursLabel. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Pull Google Trends data without an API key: interest over time, interest by region, related queries, and the Trending Now feed — for any keyword, location, time window and Google surface, cookielessly and with no proxy setup.

    What this actor does

    • Interest mode returns the same Explore view you see on trends.google.com: a 0-100 interest timeline, an interest-by-region breakdown, related topics (when Google serves them) and top and rising related queries, plus derived metrics (averageInterest, latestInterest, peakInterest, peakDate, trendChangePercent, trendDirection).
    • Comparison mode puts up to five terms on one shared 0-100 scale and also returns per-term region breakdowns and per-term related queries, so you can see how each term ranks.
    • Trending mode returns what is spiking in a country right now: rank-ordered items with the Google-reported volume bucket, growth percentage, start time and related terms, optionally with matching news headlines and links. Filter by any of Google's 18 trending categories.
    • Works with any country plus 3,400+ states, regions and metros, all nine preset time windows (including real-time now ranges) and custom date ranges down to the hour, 1,100+ categories, and all five Google surfaces (Web, Images, News, Shopping, YouTube).
    • Every row links straight back to Google Trends via exploreUrl or trendingUrl for a manual spot-check, and every row is tagged with recordType (interest or trending).

    Use cases

    • SEO and content planning — mine rising related queries and topics before writing new content.
    • Brand and market tracking — compare brands on one scale and see where interest is rising.
    • Launch monitoring — watch breaking Trending Now items with their growth and news context.
    • Market research — map demand across countries and sub-regions for a product or category.
    • Seasonality analysis — pull multi-year timelines to find recurring peaks for planning.

    Output

    Every row carries recordType, geo, geoName and scrapedAt.

    Interest rows: searchTerms, timeRange, timeRangeLabel, category, categoryName, searchType, searchTypeLabel, exploreUrl, interestOverTime (points with date, formattedTime, value — or values[] per term in comparison mode — and isPartial on the current period), averageInterest, latestInterest, peakInterest, peakDate, trendChangePercent, trendDirection, interestByRegion, relatedTopics, relatedQueries. trendDirection compares the last quarter of the window with the first (rising at +10% or more, falling at -10% or less). Comparison rows add interestByRegionPerTerm and relatedQueriesPerTerm.

    Trending rows: rank, title, hours, hoursLabel, startedAt, endedAt, volume, volumeText, growthPercent, relatedTerms, news (when includeTrendingNews is on), trendingUrl.

    A query that Google cannot serve (no data for the keyword) is logged and skipped; empty fields are omitted rather than written as nulls.

    Questions

    What do the 0-100 interest numbers mean?

    They are Google's relative interest scores, normalised to 100 at the peak of the requested window and location — never absolute search counts. Google does not publish absolute volumes; for Trending Now, volume is Google's own bucket estimate.

    Do I need an API key, cookies or a proxy?

    No. The actor works anonymously with no setup; when a request is rate-limited it waits and retries on its own, and only switches connection path if a block persists.

    Why is relatedTopics sometimes missing?

    Related topics come from a separate Google widget that currently returns no data for most queries on Google Trends itself; the field appears whenever Google serves any. Related queries are unaffected.

    How many terms can I compare at once?

    Five, Google Trends' own limit. Additional terms are trimmed. To cover more keywords, run them independently (compareMode off) — each becomes its own row on its own 0-100 scale.

    Does Trending Now work for states or cities?

    No. Google only publishes the trending list at country level; a state or metro location is mapped to its country.

    Why would a niche keyword show a flat or sparse timeline?

    Google returns points only where it has enough search data. Very rare terms yield mostly empty values; the actor reports exactly what Google sends.

    Why are trending news links only on some rows?

    Google's Trending RSS feed highlights about ten stories per country rather than every trending item, and those stories can sit anywhere in the Trending Now list. Small record caps may cover none of them; raise maxItems to cover more of the list.

    What does maxItems count?

    One interest row counts as one record and each trending item as one record. The run stops cleanly once the limit is reached.

    What are the limits of a custom date range?

    Google Trends data starts in 2004 (earlier dates are clamped to 2004), the end date cannot be in the future, and hourly ranges are limited to about seven days. Longer windows automatically switch to weekly or monthly points.

    Is this affiliated with Google?

    No. This is an independent tool and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Google.
